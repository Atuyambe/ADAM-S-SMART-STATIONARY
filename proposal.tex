\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Agriculture remains a fundamental pillar of Uganda's economy, yet smallholder farmers at Kyera Agricultural Farm continue to face challenges due to limited access to timely and accurate agricultural information. Traditional extension services are often constrained by inadequate staffing and delays in information dissemination. These gaps undermine farmers' decision-making processes, resulting in reduced productivity and increased vulnerability to pests and diseases.

With the rapid growth of mobile technologies in Uganda, there is an opportunity to improve agricultural advisory services through digital platforms. This study proposes the design and implementation of a Smart Agricultural Advisory Application that integrates localized weather forecasts with rule-based pest and disease management advisories for farmers at Kyera Agricultural Farm in Mbarara District. The application aims to bridge the disconnect between meteorological data and agricultural risks by providing preventive, weather-triggered alerts based on expert-validated correlation rules.

A mixed-methods approach incorporating interviews, questionnaires, and document review is employed to assess farmers' information needs and establish weather--pest thresholds specific to the farm's microclimate. These findings guide the development of a mobile application using an iterative prototyping methodology.

The expected outcome is a functional, user-friendly mobile application that enhances farmers' preventive decision-making and crop protection outcomes. By addressing information fragmentation through focused weather--pest integration, this pilot project contributes to modernizing agricultural advisory delivery at the farm level.

\bigskip
\textbf{Keywords:} Smart agriculture, mobile application, weather-pest integration, preventive farming, rule-based advisory, Uganda

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter:introduction}

\section{Introduction and Background of the Study}
Agriculture remains the backbone of Uganda's economy, contributing approximately 24\% of GDP and employing roughly 68\% of the workforce \cite{UBOS2023}. Despite this importance, production growth has often lagged behind population growth, raising concerns about food security and smallholder productivity \cite{AgriculturalGrowth2022}. National indices show modest improvements, but yield gaps persist for many farmers due to reactive management of pests and diseases \cite{FAO2022}.

Simultaneously, Uganda has seen a rapid increase in mobile and Internet penetration, with mobile subscriptions reaching roughly 76 per 100 people in 2023 \cite{ITU2023}. This digital growth provides a feasible channel for delivering agricultural information directly to farmers. Current digital advisory platforms in the region, however, often provide fragmented information, treating weather and pest management as separate concerns. This fragmentation misses the correlation between weather patterns and pest outbreaks, which causes preventable crop losses \cite{MAAIF2020}.

This research seeks to design and implement a Smart Agricultural Advisory Application using Kyera Agricultural Farm in Mbarara District as a case study. Kyera serves as a practical environment to pilot a mobile application focused on integrating weather-pest advisories through a rule-based engine.

\section{Problem Statement}
Smallholder farmers at Kyera Agricultural Farm rely on timely information to make productive decisions. Historically, they have depended on traditional advisory channels such as community meetings and occasional extension visits, which are often limited in frequency and precision \cite{UBOS2023}. While mobile technology adoption is rising, current digital advisory systems available to Ugandan farmers remain poorly integrated. Farmers often receive weather forecasts through one channel and pest advice through another, without a clear connection between the two \cite{WorldBank2022}.

This lack of integration leads to reactive rather than preventive pest management. Extension services face logistical challenges that prevent them from reaching all farmers with real-time, weather-informed advice \cite{MAAIF2019}. Consequently, farmers frequently receive alerts too late to take effective preventive action, resulting in persistent crop losses and income instability. Ideally, farmers should have access to an integrated mobile application that correlates weather conditions with pest management recommendations.

The core problem addressed by this study is the absence of a specialized mobile application that links localized weather forecasts with expert-validated pest/disease thresholds for farmers at Kyera Farm. This research explores how mobile technology can bridge this information gap through a rule-based advisory engine, enabling timely preventive actions and improving productivity at the farm level.

\section{Objectives of the Study}
\subsection{General Objective}
To design and implement a Smart Agricultural Advisory Application that integrates localized weather forecasts with rule-based pest and disease management advisories to enable preventive decision-making among farmers.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To identify and document expert-validated weather--pest correlation rules relevant to the crops and microclimate of Kyera Farm.
    \item To design and develop a mobile application that integrates weather forecasts with a rule-based advisory engine for pest/disease management.
    \item To test and validate the functional performance and user-acceptance of the mobile application with farmers at Kyera Agricultural Farm.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What are the expert-validated weather--pest correlation rules specific to the crops and microclimate of Kyera Agricultural Farm?
    \item How can these rules be effectively integrated into a mobile application to trigger preventive advisories?
    \item How does the use of this mobile application influence the preventive decision-making of farmers at Kyera Farm during the pilot period?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Justification of the Project}
The agricultural sector in Uganda faces significant annual crop losses due to weather-exacerbated pest outbreaks. While digital tools exist, their impact is often limited by a fragmented approach to weather and pest data. The proposed mobile application addresses this gap by providing weather-triggered alerts based on expert knowledge, transforming meteorological data into actionable intelligence for smallholder farmers.

By adopting a software-only design that leverages existing mobile infrastructure and public weather APIs, the project offers a low-cost, immediately deployable solution. This approach addresses the resource constraints common in Ugandan agriculture and provides a scalable model for other farming communities.

\subsection{Practical Impact for Farmers}
The application aims to shift farmers from reactive to preventive management, potentially reducing crop losses by providing timely alerts. This can lead to reduced production costs through optimized pesticide use and improved environmental sustainability.

\subsection{Technological Innovation}
The project focuses on a high-impact integration—weather-pest thresholds—using an accessible, equipment-free design. This demonstrates how precision agriculture benefits can be delivered without expensive hardware like IoT sensors.

\subsection{Research Contribution}
The study contributes to the documentation of weather-pest rules specific to local agroecological conditions and provides methodological insights into the development of rule-based digital extension tools in resource-constrained contexts.

\section{Scope of the Study}
The study is geographically limited to Kyera Agricultural Farm in Mbarara District, Uganda. The thematic scope is focused on integrating localized weather forecasts with rule-based pest and disease management advisories for key crops such as maize, beans, and bananas.

The application will be developed for smartphones (Android) with SMS integration for basic phone users. Functionally, it covers weather data fetching, rule-based risk assessment, and alert generation. The study will be conducted over a single cropping season. Areas such as market pricing, general crop agronomy, and IoT hardware are outside the scope of this research.

\section{Technology Infrastructure and Requirements}
\label{sec:tech-infrastructure}

The application is designed as a software-only solution, utilizing existing infrastructure to ensure feasibility:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Farmer Mobile Phones (Smartphones and feature phones)
    \item Standard Cellular Networks
    \item Public Weather APIs (e.g., OpenWeatherMap)
    \item Cloud Hosting for the backend API
\end{itemize}
The system explicitly excludes the use of physical IoT sensors or on-farm weather stations, focusing instead on data-driven integration via software.

%%% Literature Review %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{chapter:literature-review}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter reviews existing digital agricultural advisory systems and established research on weather-pest correlations. It examines the strengths and limitations of current platforms and identifies the gap this project seeks to fill.

\section{Review of Related Systems}

\subsection{EzyAgric (Uganda)}
EzyAgric provides comprehensive advisory services and farm management tools in Uganda \cite{MAAIF2020}. While it excels in data collection, there is a recognized opportunity to deepen the systematic integration between real-time weather forecasts and specific pest risk thresholds for preventive alerting.

\subsection{PlantVillage Nuru (Global)}
PlantVillage Nuru uses AI to diagnose crop diseases through smartphone cameras \cite{FAO2022}. While highly effective for identification, its current application is primarily reactive—diagnosing problems after they appear—rather than providing weather-based preventive alerts before an outbreak occurs.

\subsection{mFarms (Ghana) and iCow (Kenya)}
mFarms and iCow demonstrate the effectiveness of SMS and mobile apps in reaching smallholders \cite{FAO2022, WorldBank2021}. However, many such systems provide generic advisories that may not be dynamically triggered by localized weather-pest correlation rules.

\subsection{Rule-Based Systems in Agriculture}
Peer-reviewed research has shown that rule-based systems, which utilize expert-defined thresholds, are effective in delivering agricultural advice in regions where large historical datasets for machine learning are unavailable \cite{Saini2012}. These systems operationalize agrometeorological principles into "IF-THEN" logic that can be easily implemented in mobile applications.

\section{Research Gap}
While significant progress has been made in digital agriculture, a specific gap remains in the implementation of integrated weather--pest advisory engines accessible to Ugandan smallholders. Most current systems are either reactive or treat weather and pest advice as disconnected information streams. This project addresses the "algorithmic implementation gap" by developing a rule-based engine that correlates forecasts with expert-validated risk thresholds.

%%% Methodology %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\chapter{Methodology}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter details the prototyping methodology used to develop the mobile application, the data collection methods for identifying correlation rules, and the evaluation framework.

\section{Prototyping Methodology}
An iterative prototyping methodology is used, allowing for continuous refinement based on user and expert feedback \cite{Lewis2019}.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Requirements Analysis}: Gathering expert-validated rules and farmer needs.
    \item \textbf{Quick Design}: Designing the rule-based engine and mobile interface.
    \item \textbf{Prototype Construction}: Building the initial mobile app and backend.
    \item \textbf{User Evaluation}: Testing with farmers at Kyera Farm.
    \item \textbf{Refining}: Updating rules and UI based on feedback.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Rule-Based Advisory Engine Definition}
The application will utilize a **rule-based logic engine** derived from expert knowledge. Instead of complex statistical modeling, it will use threshold-based triggers. For example:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rule}: IF (Forecast Rainfall > 10mm) AND (Humidity > 75\%) FOR (3 consecutive days) THEN (Risk for BXW = High).
    \item \textbf{Action}: Trigger preventive advisory SMS and app notification to farmers.
\end{itemize}
This approach ensures the application is transparent, manageable, and grounded in established agricultural science.

\section{Data Collection and Sampling}
\subsection{Study Population and Sampling}
The study involves 40 participants at Kyera Farm, including farmers and agricultural experts. Purposive sampling is used to select experts for rule validation, while stratified sampling is used for farmers based on their primary crops.

\subsection{Data Collection Methods}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Interviews}: With extension officers to document pest thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Questionnaires}: To assess farmer information needs and app usability.
    \item \textbf{Document Review}: Analyzing historical records at Kyera Farm to identify recurring weather-pest patterns.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture}
The system consists of a PHP/MySQL backend that fetches weather data from an API and processes it through the rule-based engine. The mobile application, built with Flutter, serves as the primary interface for farmers to receive alerts and view advisories.

\section{Evaluation and Validation Metrics}
The mobile application will be evaluated based on:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Functional Performance}: Using metrics such as **Precision** (percentage of alerts that were relevant) and **Recall** (percentage of actual risk periods correctly identified).
    \item \textbf{User Acceptance}: Measured through the System Usability Scale (SUS) during farmer testing.
\end{itemize}
If no outbreaks occur during the pilot season, validation will rely on "simulated risk" scenarios where farmers evaluate the clarity and perceived usefulness of alerts triggered by historical weather data.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
Informed consent will be obtained from all participants. The application will explicitly state that advisories are based on expert rules and are intended to support, not replace, professional agricultural advice.
